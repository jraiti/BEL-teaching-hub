UQ Assessment Principles

Add descriptor here

  1. Assessment is a developmental activity
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Engage students in timely, meaningful learning activities.

    ● Develop students’ capacity to judge the quality of their own and others’ work.

    ● Select assessment forms based on suitability for intended learning outcomes.

    ● Minimum weighting of 10% for any assessment item.

    ● 2–4 summative assessment items per course; one may be a sequenced item worth ≥10%.

    ● When pedagogically justified, more than 4 items (including another sequence) may be considered by the BEL‑ADA.

    ● No marks for attendance; focus on performance quality (e.g., quality of participation or contribution to group outcomes).

    Back to top
  2. Assessment involves mutual responsibility
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Orient students to assessment methods and standards.

    ● Provide timely opportunities for students to demonstrate learning.

    ● Support learning progression through effective feedback.

    ● Help students understand their responsibility to evidence.

    • Use student‑friendly descriptions of assessment items and submission requirements.
    • Enable sense‑making of criteria/standards so students can judge their own efforts.
    • Provide meaningful, timely feedback (aim for a 2‑week turnaround).
    • Use early, formative (“low stakes”) assessment where appropriate (e.g., by Census date).
    • Peer review is required for all team tasks.
    • Variance only in consultation with the Program Convenor.
    Back to top
  3. Assessment is criterion‑referenced
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Judge each student’s performance against explicit and predetermined criteria and standards.

    ● Specify and explain criteria and standards for each assessment item.

    ● Make criteria sheets accessible by the end of Week 1.

    ● Progressively moderate to ensure consistent judgements across the cohort.

    ● Ideally, two markers assess performance/presentation items together; otherwise record the item.

    ● Award final grades in line with the UQ Grading System.

    ● Enable demonstration across a range of competencies (e.g., 1–7 scale or equivalent).

    Back to top
  4. Assessment is transparent
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Ensure explicit, logical relationships between items, criteria/standards, and grades.

    ● Align criteria sheets to support the completion of set tasks.

    ● Achieve consistent, fair, reliable, and valid results.

    ● Hurdles must serve a genuine purpose; link to an item only after consultation and approval by the Program Leader.

    ● Tasks must enable demonstration at all grade levels (e.g., 7, 6, 5).

    Back to top
  5. Assessment is valid
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Reliably measure intended learning outcomes with consistent evidence of performance.

    ● Design items that generate valid evidence of learning.

    ● Weightings reflect the importance of outcomes assessed.

    ● Avoid over‑assessing the same outcomes.

    ● Short‑response items (e.g., MCQ) limited to 25%* of overall assessment; use less frequently at higher levels of study.

    ● Prevent passing grades via memorisation/reproduction of previous items.

    ● Provide a range of learning opportunity from minimum to highest standards.

    *Variance only in consultation with the Program Convenor.

    Back to top
  6. Assessment is authentic
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Provide appropriate, meaningful, and worthwhile accomplishments.

    ● >Where possible, require demonstration of knowledge/skills used in real‑world settings.

    ● Ensure items authentically reflect intended learning outcomes.

    ● Design group assessment only where the task requires genuine collaboration.

    Back to top
  7. Assessment is fair and inclusive
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Respect and accommodate diverse needs so students can engage with outcomes.

    ● Support equitable engagement with content, format, and conduct; avoid unfair advantage/disadvantage.

    ● Vary forms of assessment across the Program and within courses.

    ● No single item (including sequences; excluding theses/projects) exceeds 60% of the final grade.

    ● Do not use a single form (e.g., exams/quizzes/tests) to assess all learning outcomes.

    ● Students can apply for extensions [See UQBS resource: ECP Guidelines].

    Back to top
  8. Assessment is a purposeful, professional activity
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Integrate assessment into course/program design, review, and evaluation.

    ● Ensure quality assurance and moderation at course, school, and faculty levels.

    ● Collaborate to provide whole‑of‑program coherence.

    ● Commit to ongoing revision and enhancement of practice.

    ● Course Coordinators liaise with Program Leaders so ECPs reflect intended experiences.

    ● At semester end, Course Coordinators submit course reflections to Program Leaders [See UQBS resource: Course Reflections]

    Back to top
  9. Assessment is sustainable and workable
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Resource assessment so it is achievable for teachers and students.

    ● Design relative to available and appropriate resources.

    ● Direct additional resources to courses trialling/implementing innovations.

    ● Align word/time limits with expectations and weighting.

    ● No School‑based examinations (including in‑class quizzes) during the final two teaching weeks.

    ● No assessment held or due during the revision period [See UQ Academic Calendar].

    Back to top
  10. Assessment is secure
    Assessment Design In Practice

    ● Provide a verified measure of student performance at the program level.

    ● Help students uphold integrity and ethical standards.

    ● Design and conduct assessment with due regard to integrity and security to minimise misconduct.

    ● See Assessment Security Guidelines. Updated guidance is provided on the AI Teacher Hub.

    Back to top

Secure Assessment

Ensuring academic integrity and authentic student performance

Secure Assessment Strategies

  • Supervised Tasks: Invigilated exams and in‑class assessments
  • Secure Questioning: Student discussions about submitted work in supervised conditions
  • Scaffolded Submission: Samples of work developed in supervised settings
  • Real‑time Completion: Entire tasks completed under supervision
  • Viva Voce: Oral examination components
  • Corroboration: Work verified by trusted observers

Assessment Hurdles

Hurdles are assessment requirements that must be satisfied to receive a specific grade. Use the three principles:

1. Critical Competency & Security: Add hurdles only when essential for learning outcomes or to securely assure professional competencies

2. Reasonable Standards: Set achievable thresholds that demonstrate competency without unnecessary stress

3. Clear Communication: Explicitly state hurdle requirements, rationale, and consequences in course profiles from day one

🛠️ Tools & Resources

Open Assessment (AI‑Permitted)

Students can use AI tools transparently with proper acknowledgment. Design considerations:

  • Set clear expectations about AI acknowledgment requirements in course profile
  • Model effective AI use through scaffolded prompts and examples
  • Use Microsoft Copilot to simulate AI's role in completing tasks
  • Design tasks requiring synthesis, application, and critical thinking beyond AI capabilities
  • Consider tasks that ask students to evaluate, critique, or improve AI‑generated content

Secure Assessment (AI‑Restricted)

Assessment designed to verify independent student learning without AI assistance:

  • Supervised tasks: In‑class exams, invigilated assessments
  • Oral components: Viva voce, presentations with Q&A
  • Real‑time demonstrations: Practical skills, live problem‑solving
  • Scaffolded submissions: Work developed progressively in supervised settings

⚠️ Important Policy Update: From Semester 2, 2025, every course must include at least one secure assessment item. Plan your assessment strategy accordingly.
